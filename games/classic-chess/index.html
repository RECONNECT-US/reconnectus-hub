How to play chess

The aim of the game

Put your opponent’s king in checkmate: their king is under attack and they have no legal way to escape. You never capture the king.

Getting started

White moves first, then players take turns making one move. Each side begins with a king, a queen, two rooks, two bishops, two knights and eight pawns. White starts at the bottom of this board.

Select one of your pieces, then select the square you want to move to. Capture an opposing piece by moving onto its square. You cannot land on one of your own pieces or make a move that leaves your king in check.

How each piece moves

King
One square in any direction. It cannot move onto a square attacked by an opponent. The two kings can never stand next to each other.
Queen
Any number of squares in a straight line: horizontally, vertically or diagonally.
Rook
Any number of squares horizontally or vertically.
Bishop
Any number of squares diagonally. Each bishop always stays on the same colour of square.
Knight
In an L shape: two squares in one direction and one square sideways. It is the only piece that can jump over other pieces.
Pawn
One square forward into an empty square. From its starting square, it may move two squares if both are empty. It captures one square diagonally forward, never straight ahead, and cannot move backwards. White pawns move up this board; Black pawns move down.

Except for the knight, pieces cannot jump over other pieces. A capture ends the move on the captured piece’s square, with the special en passant exception below.

Check and checkmate

Check means your king is under attack. You must get out of check by moving the king, capturing the attacking piece or blocking the attack when possible. You cannot block a knight’s attack.

Checkmate means there is no legal escape from check. The player delivering checkmate wins. In this game, check gives a brief board flash and checkmate brings falling confetti.

Three special moves

  • Castling: move your king two squares towards a rook; the rook moves to the square the king crossed. Neither piece may have moved before, all squares between them must be empty, and the king cannot be in check, cross an attacked square or finish in check. Select the king, then its destination to castle.
  • Promotion: when a pawn reaches the far end of the board, choose a queen, rook, bishop or knight. You can choose a piece even if you already have one of that type.
  • En passant: if an opposing pawn moves two squares from its starting square and lands beside your pawn, your pawn may capture it as though it moved only one square. Move diagonally onto the square it passed over. You must do this on your very next move or the opportunity is lost.

When the game is a draw

  • Stalemate: the player to move has no legal move, but their king is not in check.
  • Insufficient material: neither side can possibly deliver checkmate, such as king against king, or king and one bishop or knight against a lone king.
  • Threefold repetition: the same position occurs three times, with the same player to move and the same possible moves.
  • Fifty-move rule: each player makes 50 moves without a pawn move or a capture.

This game ends automatically when it detects one of these draws. In standard over-the-board chess, threefold repetition and the fifty-move rule normally require a player to claim the draw.

Your learner account and saved game

On the RECONNECT-US website, sign in using your existing account. The game loads your learner profile and resumes your saved position. Moves, captures, undo, play mode and finished-game records save automatically. Wait for Saved to your account before moving to another device.

If your connection drops after loading, the game keeps a device copy and retries when you reconnect. If you open this file directly from your computer, you play as a guest with a separate device save. Guest games are not added to a learner account. On shared devices, sign out of RECONNECT-US when finished. A completed game can still be undone; its result is finalised in your history when you start a new game.

Using this chess game

  • Two player: two people take turns on the same board.
  • Computer: you play White and the computer plays Black.
  • Beginner: shows pointers for legal destinations when you select a piece.
  • Normal: hides the pointers. The same chess rules still apply.
  • Undo: takes back a move. Against the computer, it takes you back to your previous turn.
  • Captured pieces: White pieces taken by Black appear above the board; Black pieces taken by White appear below it.
  • Full screen: expands the game. Use the button again or press Escape to leave full screen.

Close this guide or press Escape to return to your game. Your position is kept, and the computer waits while this guide is open.
